Start here: the basic idea in four sentences.

1. Water in the brain moves randomly, and this is called diffusion. 2. In white matter, the walls of nerve fibres make water move easily along the fibre and poorly across it. 3. A special MRI (DWI) measures how freely water moves in many directions, and a tensor summarises this for every tiny cube (voxel) of brain. 4. Tractography follows the preferred direction from voxel to voxel to draw an estimate of the fibre pathway.

Key terms (tap to open)

Diffusion and voxel

Diffusion is the random movement of water molecules. A voxel is one tiny cube of the 3D image, typically about 2 mm on a side, and each voxel gets its own measurement.

b0 image and b-value

The b0 image is a scan with no diffusion weighting: the "before" picture. The b-value (s/mm²) is the sensitivity dial. About 1000 is standard for DTI. Higher values are more sensitive to diffusion but noisier.

Gradient directions

Each diffusion-weighted image tests water movement along one direction. DTI needs at least 6, and 30 or more are used in practice so that noise averages out. Lab 3 below shows why.

Tensor, λ1 and v1

The tensor is a 3×3 table that describes the diffusion shape in a voxel, like a ball or a rugby ball. Its three eigenvalues (λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ λ3) are the diffusion along its three natural axes, and v1, the direction of the largest one, is the presumed fibre direction.

FA (fractional anisotropy)

How directional the diffusion is, from 0 (a perfect ball) to 1 (a needle). Healthy white matter is high, grey matter lower, CSF near zero. FA also falls with edema, infiltration and damage.

MD (mean diffusivity)

The average diffusion in all directions, meaning how freely water moves overall. It is raised in edema and necrosis and reduced in densely cellular tissue.

Colour-coded direction map

The standard colours: red = left-right (e.g. corpus callosum), green = front-back (e.g. cingulum), blue = up-down (e.g. corticospinal tract). Wrong colours are a quick sign of flipped gradient axes.

Tractography, seed, ROI

Tractography links voxel directions into streamlines. A seed is where tracking starts, and an ROI (region of interest) is a region used to start, include or exclude streamlines. Streamlines are estimates of fibre pathways, not actual axons.

Crossing fibres and CSD

In many voxels two or more bundles cross. A single tensor cannot represent this, so it gives a muddled direction. CSD and other multi-fibre models estimate several directions per voxel and handle crossings better, but they need better data (more directions, higher b-values).

Know the limits before you trust a tract

⚠️ Common pitfalls

  • A missing tract is not proof of missing fibres. Edema and infiltration lower FA, so tracking stops early.
  • A clean tract is not proof of function. It shows structure, not whether the fibres still work.
  • Settings change the answer. Always check a stricter and a looser threshold.
  • Distortion and misregistration can shift tracts by millimetres.
  • Brain shift after craniotomy makes pre-operative tracts less accurate.

Rotate the green scan direction (or press Sweep). Scanning along the fibre loses signal; scanning across keeps it. Set the b-value to 0 and the contrast vanishes.
